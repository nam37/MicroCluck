1Summary

MicroCluck is a ~25 cm bipedal walking robot chicken in the style of Pollen Robotics' Microduck. Its whole torso is a vertical, front-facing Freenove ESP32-S3 3.5" touchscreen (FNK0104 family) showing an animated chicken face, with a comb on top, tail feathers at the back and three-toed feet. It has no separate head, neck or beak.

Walking comes from a reinforcement-learning policy trained in simulation (forked from Pollen's open-source microduck_rl) and run entirely on the ESP32-S3 at 50 Hz. It is driven from a phone browser over the robot's own Wi-Fi. A claw on top is planned for v2.

Two decisions shape everything else:

  • Single MCU. The ESP32-S3 runs the control loop, policy inference, servo bus, IMU fusion, face, sound, Wi-Fi controller and OTA. No Raspberry Pi.
  • Simulation first. The robot is designed, trained and driven end to end in simulation, including the real firmware logic and face, before most parts are ordered (§3).

The name is a homage to Microduck. MicroCluck is a personal project and is not affiliated with Pollen Robotics or Hugging Face.

  • Camera or vision, voice interaction, ToF or obstacle sensing, autonomous navigation
  • Rough terrain
  • Standing up after a fall (stretch goal for v1.1)
  • Direct Bluetooth gamepad pairing. The ESP32-S3 is BLE-only, so physical controllers connect through the phone (§9.5).
  • Any commercial use. Microduck's meshes are licensed CC BY-NC-SA (§4).

3Development approach

Work runs in two phases. Phase A builds MicroCluck in simulation: the model, the walking policy, a design validation report, the C port of the policy and a virtual MicroCluck that runs the real firmware logic and face against the simulator. Phase B builds the robot.

Bench bring-up (H0) needs only the Freenove board, which is already owned, so it runs alongside Phase A. Servos, battery and power parts are ordered at the design freeze after S3, once the simulator has settled stance width, mass and torque margins. Two kinds of hardware work may start earlier: driver bring-up on one or two early servos and an IMU (the start of H1 and H2), and fit-check prints of the board mount and bezel on the board you already own (the start of H3). The full exit criteria of H1 to H3 still wait for the freeze.

4Reference design and constraints

The design stays deliberately close to Microduck so its training stack, actuator model and leg kinematics can be reused.

ItemMicroduck (reference)MicroCluck

Facts carried over from Pollen's repos

  • Policies are trained at 50 Hz with PPO and exported to ONNX. The exporter bakes the observation normalizer into the ONNX graph.
  • The actor sees 61 values: 48 proprioception plus commands for twist (3), head pose (4) and body pose (6).
  • Actuator fidelity is the dominant sim2real gap at this scale. The XL330 is modeled down to its voltage control law, with randomized battery voltage, voltage sag, command delay and friction. Each task has a Backlash variant with ±1° of gear play.
  • Training needs a CUDA GPU; Pollen quotes 1–2 hours for a usable gait at 4096 parallel environments.
  • Robot models are exported from Onshape with onshape-to-robot.
  • A simulated duck can run the real robot software against a body in MuJoCo, which is the model for the virtual MicroCluck (§8).
  • Licensing: code is Apache-2.0; STL meshes are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). The CAD is not published, and Pollen asks that the mechanical design not be called open-source hardware.

XL330-M288-T key specs

18 g. Stall torque 0.52 N·m at 5 V (1.47 A). Input 3.7–6.0 V, 5.0 V recommended. TTL half-duplex bus with 3.3 V logic (5 V tolerant), 9.6 kbps to 4 Mbps, Protocol 2.0. Feedback includes position, velocity, current, temperature and input voltage. 12-bit absolute encoder. The estimated continuous torque is only ~0.10 N·m, 20% of stall, so thermal margin is a design constraint (§7.2, §9.8).

6.3Servo bus interface

  • The gate drives the bus only while DIR is high, then releases the line so servos can reply.
  • RX also hears the ESP32's own transmissions. The dxl driver discards that echo, or the UART's RS-485 mode is configured to ignore RX during TX.
  • 3.3 V logic matches the XL330's TTL level, so no level shifting is needed. Unused gates: inputs and enables tied to GND.
  • Built on perfboard with DIP parts and a socket for the chip. There is no surface-mount soldering anywhere in the build.

6.4Power

  • Servo rail: regulated 5.0 V, the XL330's recommended voltage. Keep it inside the voltage randomization range used in training.
  • Peak current: the theoretical worst case is 10 × 1.47 A ≈ 15 A with every servo stalled. Size the buck for at least 8 A peak, add bulk capacitance at the bus entry and fuse the pack.
  • Logic rail: a separate 5 V buck into the Freenove board, with grounds joined at one star point.
  • Monitoring: INA226 on the pack; the servos' Present Input Voltage register for the servo rail.

6.5Mechanical

CAD starts in S1, because the model's masses and inertias come from it, and is finished in H3 once S3 has settled the stance width.

  • Legs: reuse Microduck's leg geometry from the STLs in microduck_rl (non-commercial license) to keep kinematic parity with Pollen's model. Only the torso and feet change.
  • Torso: screen frame with a raised bezel and rubber corner bumpers, glass recessed 2–3 mm. Forward falls land on the screen. Bezel in yellow, per the renders.
  • Comb: snap-on piece on a top mounting boss. The v2 claw uses the same boss and replaces the comb, or the comb becomes the claw's cover.
  • Tail: feathers in slightly flexible TPU, so the tail doubles as a bumper for backward falls and a third contact point when sitting. It shifts the center of mass backward, so its mass and collision shape go into the model.
  • Feet: three-toed chicken feet on a flat sole, which is the designed contact surface. Shorten the inner toe, or include the toes in the collision model so the policy learns to avoid foot-to-foot contact on narrow steps.
  • Stance: hip spacing is decided in simulation (§7.2). Wider spacing stands steadier but makes each step's side-to-side weight shift larger.
  • IMU: rigidly mounted to the torso frame. Its exact pose relative to the model's base link goes into the model.
  • Cabling: daisy-chain routing with strain relief across the hip joints.

7Simulation and training

7.1Fork and adapt microduck_rl

  1. Fork pollen-robotics/microduck_rl and include it as the sim/ submodule. Reproduce Pollen's stock walking policy first (S0).
  2. Model the torso, feet, comb and tail in Onshape if possible and export with onshape-to-robot, the same pipeline Pollen uses, so masses and inertias come from the CAD. A hand-edited box torso is acceptable for a first pass.
  3. Create MicroCluck versions of the robot models (robot_walk.xml, robot_groundcontact.xml and their backlash variants).
  4. Remove the neck and head joints (neck_pitch, head_pitch, head_yaw, head_roll) and the beak.
  5. Add the torso with measured or estimated mass, center of mass and inertia for the board, frame, battery, comb and tail. Give the tail a collision shape.
  6. Replace the feet with the MicroCluck sole (and toes, if kept long). Set the hip spacing.
  7. Change the joint count from 14 to 10 everywhere: actuators, observations, actions, joint-index constants and the repo's regression tests, which lock in joint-index mappings. Remove head-pose commands and their reward terms.
  8. Observation contract v1 (proposed, 39 values): gyro (3), projected gravity (3), joint positions (10), joint velocities (10), last action (10), twist command (3). Confirm the composition of Pollen's 48-value proprioception block in mdp.py before freezing it.
  9. Actor network: a small MLP, target 256-128-64 with ELU, about 52k parameters or 208 KB as float32. The critic can be any size because it is never deployed.
  10. Domain randomization: keep Pollen's defaults and add torso mass and center of mass ±10–15% for measurement error, plus a 0–100 g payload at the top of the torso so v1 walking survives the v2 claw.
  11. Train the Backlash variants. Reduce num-envs if the 3060's 12 GB runs out.

v1 tasks: Velocity-Flat, where standing is a zero command. v1.1: VelStand for walking plus fall recovery, and SitStand, which can use the tail as a support point. v1 still needs a sit for the dead-man, low-battery and over-temperature stops, but as a scripted joint trajectory (§9.7) rather than a policy, so it costs no training. S3 checks it (§7.2).

7.2Design validation (S3)

Before any parts beyond the board are ordered, the simulator answers the mechanical questions. Results go in docs/design-report.md, and each decision becomes an ADR.

CheckMethodPass condition
Thermal marginRMS torque per joint over a 60 s walk at the top commanded speedEvery joint at or below the XL330's ~0.10 N·m continuous rating at normal walking speed
Peak torquePeak torque per joint across walking, pushes and payloadBelow ~70% of the 0.52 N·m stall torque
Stance widthTrain and compare 2–3 hip spacingsBest combination of push recovery and energy use (sum of |torque × velocity|)
Toe clearanceMinimum distance between the feet during the gaitNo foot-to-foot contacts
Claw headroomLargest top payload that still tracks commandsAt least 60 g, the low end of the claw reserve
Mass budgetSum of modeled parts≤ 800 g for v1
Scripted sitReplay the sit and stand-up trajectories (§9.7) in the model and compute static torque along themRests stably on the feet and tail; peak static torque on the way back up stays below ~70% of the stall torque

If a joint fails the thermal check, the fixes in order of preference are: reduce mass, shorten the leg's lever arms, then consider a stronger servo for that joint. A different servo model needs its own actuator identification, so it is the last resort.

7.3Policy export pipeline

Lives in tools/policy_export/.

  1. Input is the ONNX file from Pollen's scripts/export.py, with the normalizer baked in. Never convert a checkpoint by hand.
  2. Extract weights, biases, activations and the normalizer. Fold the normalizer into the first layer: W' = W / σ and b' = b − W·(μ/σ).
  3. Emit policy_<name>.h with C arrays and metadata: input and output sizes, action scale, default pose and the contract hash.
  4. Generate golden vectors: at least 1000 plausible observations and their ONNX outputs.
  5. Device test: C inference against the golden vectors, maximum absolute error ≤ 1e-4.
  6. Closed-loop test: the same C inference, built for the host, drives MuJoCo. The gait must match the ONNX policy's.

7.4Sim2real contract

docs/sim2real-contract.md is the single, versioned, hashed source of truth. It must match Pollen's runtime (robotd) and constants (microduck_constants.py) exactly:

  • Observation order, units and scaling
  • Where joint velocity comes from: the servo register or a finite difference of position
  • Action post-processing: action scale, default pose offset, clipping
  • Servo control mode and firmware PD gains, since the sim emulates the servo's own PD loop
  • Command ranges, which the phone commands are clamped to
  • Loop rate (50 Hz) and expected command delay

Firmware refuses to enable torque if a policy's contract hash doesn't match its build.

8Virtual MicroCluck

The virtual MicroCluck runs the real firmware logic against the simulator on your computer. The phone connects to it exactly as it would to the robot, and the face renders in a desktop window with eyes stabilized by the simulated IMU. Everything except the hardware drivers is developed and tested here first, and it remains the regression harness afterwards.

8.1Hardware abstraction layer

The firmware core (control loop, state machine, safety, policy inference, face logic and protocol handling) is plain C with no ESP-IDF includes. It talks to the world only through this interface, with one implementation for the ESP32 and one for the host.

InterfaceCalls
Servo bussync read of positions and velocities, sync write of goal positions, torque enable, round-robin health read
IMUlatest gyro and gravity vector
Clockmonotonic microseconds, 50 Hz tick (sim time on the host)
Display and inputLVGL display and touch drivers
Audioplay clip, set volume
Networkprotocol messages in and out
Powerpack voltage and current, servo rail voltage

8.2Host twin

  • A Python harness runs MuJoCo with the MicroCluck model and the same BAM XL330 actuator model used in training.
  • It loads the firmware core as a shared library and calls the control step at 50 Hz of simulated time.
  • A WebSocket server implements the §9.5 protocol, so the phone's controller page works unchanged on the local network.
  • The face runs in LVGL's SDL desktop port, built from the same UI source. Whether it shares the process or runs alongside is an ADR.
  • Battery voltage, Wi-Fi drops, pushes and a stalled control task can be injected to exercise safety behavior. The simulated servo bus implements the Bus Watchdog, using the behavior H1 measures on real servos.
  • The twin writes the same log format as the robot, so twin and hardware runs can be compared side by side in H5.

8.3Fidelity requirements

  • Servo positions quantized like the 12-bit encoder; velocity in the register's units, or derived as the contract specifies
  • Bus latency and command delay matching the timing budget (§9.3)
  • IMU noise and bias added to the simulated gyro and gravity vector
  • A deterministic mode with fixed seeds for automated tests
  • A headless mode for running the safety and regression tests in CI

9Firmware

9.1Stack

  • ESP-IDF v5.x, C (or C++), FreeRTOS
  • The hardware-independent core builds for ESP-IDF and for the host with CMake (§8.1)
  • LVGL via esp_lvgl_port; ESP-DSP for the matrix math
  • esp_http_server with WebSocket support; static assets in LittleFS
  • I2S driver and the onboard codec for sound effects
  • Native OTA with A/B partitions and rollback; SD card logging

ESP-IDF over Arduino for explicit core pinning and priorities, the RS-485 half-duplex UART mode, esp_timer precision and control of the OTA partition table.

9.4Memory plan

  • Internal SRAM: Wi-Fi and LwIP, task stacks, DMA-capable display buffers, I2S buffers, bus and IMU buffers.
  • PSRAM: non-DMA LVGL buffers, face accessories, sound clips (or stream from LittleFS or SD), policy weights at ~208 KB per behavior, the log ring buffer.
  • Measure inference with weights in PSRAM first. Move the active policy to internal SRAM or quantize to int8 only if it exceeds 5 ms.
  • Report heap high-water marks in telemetry.

9.5Phone controller and protocol

Networking. The robot runs its own access point by default: SSID microcluck-XXXX with WPA2, at 192.168.4.1. Joining home Wi-Fi is an option for development, with mDNS at microcluck.local. The host twin listens on the computer's own address.

Pages.

  • / is the controller, served from flash: a joystick for forward and strafe, a turn control, behavior and expression buttons, and an E-stop. It reads the browser's Gamepad API, so any controller paired to the phone (PS5, Xbox) works.
  • /status and /settings are server-rendered fragments, HTMX-style, for configuration, mode, Wi-Fi, gains and volume.
  • /ota uploads firmware and policies.

Dead-man. No cmd for 300 ms sets the velocity command to zero, so MicroCluck stands still. No connection for 5 s sits it down with the scripted SIT (§9.7).

9.6Face, sound and touch

A flat vector chicken face on a black screen: big white eyes with dark pupils, small arched brows, an orange beak and pink cheeks. The beak stays fixed; the eyes, brows and accessories carry the expression. The face fills the upper two-thirds of the 320 × 480 portrait screen, and a slim status strip in the lower third shows battery, Wi-Fi and mode.

Curious, happy, surprised and love come from the renders; dizzy, confused, sleepy and error are first sketches in the same style. Event expressions (surprised, love) briefly override the mode's default.

Face rig. The face is drawn from LVGL shapes (circles, arcs, triangles), not stored bitmaps. Each expression is a preset of these parameters:

  • per eye: openness (0–1), scale, pupil offset, lid curve from open to the happy arc
  • brow angle on each side
  • beak opening (0–1)
  • cheek strength
  • accessory: none, heart, sparks, question mark, Zzz or spiral

Transitions tween over 150–300 ms. Blinks fire every 3–6 seconds at random. Gaze follows the turn and strafe commands. Small accessory icons may be images in PSRAM.

Stabilized eyes. Like a real chicken's head, the face holds still while the body moves. The whole face shifts opposite to torso roll (horizontally) and pitch (vertically) using the gravity vector from imu_task, low-pass filtered and clamped to about ±20 px. It is off when fallen or E-stopped. Only the face's bounding box redraws, at no more than 30 fps.

Sound. The onboard codec and speaker (to confirm on the 3.5") play short clips from LittleFS or SD through audio_task: a cluck when it starts walking and occasionally while walking (rate-limited, not every step), a startled squawk on a bump or fall, a happy cluck when petted and a sleepy coo on low battery or long idle. Volume and mute live in /settings. Sound must never affect control-loop timing.

Touch. A tap triggers love. A long press is an E-stop and turns torque off.

9.7Mode state machine

SIT. A scripted crouch onto the feet and tail, not a policy: joint targets interpolated over about 2 s at low gains, so v1 needs no extra training. It is entered from STAND on a sit command, after 5 s without a connection, on a servo over-temperature (S4) and as the first step of a low-battery stop (S5). WALK stops first, because the command goes to zero, and then sits. Standing up again goes back through STAND_INIT. SIT holds torque at low gains and drops to IDLE with torque off after a timeout, 30 s by default. The v1.1 SitStand policy replaces the script later.

Enable only when upright. STAND_INIT eases the legs to the home pose from wherever they happen to be, which is a bad idea with the robot on its back or held at an angle. IDLE to STAND_INIT is refused unless the gravity vector puts the torso within 20° of vertical (default, tunable). The robot answers the phone with a refusal and the reason, and the status strip shows it (S10).

9.8Safety requirements

IDRequirement
S1E-stop from the phone or a long press on the screen turns torque off within one control cycle.
S2Dead-man: 300 ms without a command sets velocity to zero.
S3Fall detection per §9.7, with torque off or limp to protect the gears and screen.
S4Servo health: round-robin reads of temperature and hardware error status. Over the temperature threshold, sit and rest (SIT); on a hardware error, including a Bus Watchdog error (S9), torque off and report.
S5Low battery (INA226): warn, then sit (SIT) and turn torque off before the BMS cuts out.
S6Phone commands are clamped to the policy's training ranges.
S7A policy whose contract hash doesn't match the build cannot enable torque.
S8S1–S7 and S10, and the software side of S9, are covered by automated tests on the host twin.
S9Hang protection. A task watchdog covers control_task and imu_task, and a stall resets the MCU. When torque is enabled, every servo's Bus Watchdog (register 98, 20 ms units) is set to about 100 ms, so a hung or crashed MCU leaves the servos stopped within roughly 100 ms instead of replaying the last goal. The e-manual says only that the servo stops on expiry, and the error blocks new goals until it is cleared, so H1 measures what torque does afterwards. After any reset, the first bus action is to clear the Bus Watchdog (write 0) and broadcast torque off. The power switch stays reachable as the last resort.
S10Enable only when upright: IDLE to STAND_INIT is refused unless the torso is within 20° of vertical by the gravity vector. The refusal and its reason reach the phone and the status strip.

10Milestones

Each milestone meets its exit criteria before the next one in its track starts. H0 runs in parallel with Phase A. Before the design freeze only two things may start: driver bring-up on one or two early servos and an IMU (the start of H1 and H2) and fit-check prints (the start of H3). The full exit criteria of H1 to H3 wait for the freeze. H1, H2 and H3 do not depend on each other and can overlap; H4 needs all three, and H5 also waits for S5.

11v2 claw

  • Claw servos join the same Dynamixel bus, so no new pins are needed.
  • It mounts on the comb's top boss; the comb is removed or reworked as the claw's cover.
  • Option A: a fixed gripper on top of the torso for carrying things handed to it. Simplest.
  • Option B: a one- or two-joint arm (shoulder pitch and gripper) that swings forward and down. Floor pickup needs a crouch-and-reach policy, like Microduck's GroundPick task with its beak.
  • v1 prepares for it with the shared boss, the mass reserve and payload randomization in training (§7.1).
  • Total mass stays at or below ~850 g; the XL330s are sized for Microduck's ~800 g.

Sim2real gapSame servos as Pollen's actuator model, backlash training, measured mass properties, the sim2real contract, identical logs from twin and robot.
Mass estimates are wrongWeigh the board now; mass randomization covers estimation error; H3 regenerates the model from the final CAD, H4 updates it with weighed parts, and S2 is retrained if needed.
Mechanical design takes longer than plannedCAD starts in S1 and is the source of the model. Fit-check prints on the board you own before the freeze. H3 has its own exit criteria.
Firmware hang leaves the servos drivenTask watchdog, Bus Watchdog on every servo, torque off after any reset, a reachable power switch (S9).
Robot enabled lying down or held at an angleEnable is refused unless the torso is upright (S10).
Reward tuning eats timeStart from Pollen's tuned task; change one thing at a time from the stock duck.
The twin is too idealizedEncoder quantization, bus latency, IMU noise and command delay in the host HAL (§8.3).
3.5" pinout differs from the 2.8"Verify in H0. Remap through the GPIO matrix; worst case, poll the IMU instead of using its interrupt.
Wi-Fi interrupts cause loop jitterCore separation; measured in H1 under full load.
Stabilized face forces big redrawsRedraw only the face's bounding box, cap at 30 fps, clamp the offset; measure core 0 load and reduce if needed.

13Open questions

  1. 3.5" FNK0104 details: exact pinout, touch controller and address, PSRAM size, 5 V input location, and whether the audio codec and amp match the 2.8".
  2. Which XL330 variant (M288 or M077) Pollen's actuator model targets. Check microduck_constants.py.
  3. The exact composition of Pollen's 48-value proprioception block and their action post-processing (mdp.py, robotd).
  4. IMU choice. The ICM-42688-P is currently supply-constrained, with at least one breakout seller citing severe shortages and raised prices. Alternatives: LSM6DSOX or LSM6DSV16X with on-MCU fusion, or BNO085 with on-chip fusion (its I2C has known quirks on the ESP32).
  5. Operating system on the RTX 3060 machine. MuJoCo Warp and mjlab are Linux-first; a Windows machine likely needs WSL2.
  6. CAD tool. Onshape is recommended for the onshape-to-robot pipeline.
  7. Twin process layout: LVGL in the same process as the harness or alongside it.
  8. Battery choice and target runtime.
  9. Reuse Microduck's leg STLs as they are, or redesign.
  10. Firmware language: C (default), C++ or Rust (esp-rs).
  11. Final designs for the dizzy, confused, sleepy and error faces.

15Notes for Claude Code

  • Phase A comes first. H0 bench work, early driver bring-up on one or two servos and fit-check prints may run in parallel. The full exit criteria of H1 to H3, and everything after them, wait for the design freeze (§3).
  • Work milestone by milestone and don't start the next until the exit criteria are met (§10).
  • Code in firmware/components/core never includes ESP-IDF headers. It reaches hardware only through the HAL (§8.1), and every core change must build and pass tests on the twin.
  • docs/sim2real-contract.md is the source of truth for observation and action layout. Firmware, twin and sim must reference the same contract hash.
  • docs/face-spec.md is the source of truth for expression presets and the state-to-expression mapping.
  • Never hand-edit generated policy headers; regenerate them with tools/policy_export.
  • Nothing in control_task blocks or allocates. The face and audio must never affect its timing.
  • Training runs on the RTX 3060 machine (CUDA). Playback, the twin and firmware work run on the Mac.
  • Hardware-in-the-loop steps (wiring, soldering, flashing, gantry tests, weighing parts) are manual. Flag them for Neil rather than assuming results.
  • Record significant decisions as ADRs in docs/decisions/.
